# Ad Click Aggregator

## Prompt
Collect and aggregate ad clicks so advertisers can measure the performance of their campaigns. Ads are shown on a website or app.

## Functional requirements
- The user clicks and is redirected to the advertiser's website.
- Advertisers query click metrics over time with a minimum granularity of 1 minute.
- Out of scope: targeting, ad serving, cross-device, offline marketing.

## Non-functional requirements
- Peak of 10k clicks/s.
- Analytics queries in under 1 s.
- Fault tolerant, without losing clicks.
- As close to real time as possible.
- Idempotent tracking (do not count the same click twice).
- Out of scope: fraud, demographic/geo profiles, conversions.

## Estimations
- 10M active ads; peak 10k clicks/s (~10x the average of ~1k/s); ~100M clicks/day.
- 5 min batch: ~3M events x <=100 B ≈ 300 MB.
- Idempotency cache: 100M x 16 B ≈ 1.6 GB/day in the worst case.

## API
Input: `/click` records and redirects. Output: metrics per advertiser (no formal API).

## Data model
- Raw events: EventId, AdId, UserId, Timestamp.
- Analytics table: AdId, MinuteTimestamp, UniqueClicks (`COUNT(*)`/`COUNT(DISTINCT UserId)` queries by AdId and range).
- Impression ID (deep dive 3).

## Architecture (high-level design)
1. **Click -> redirect** ([[http-redirects]]): client-side redirect (URL sent with the ad and POST to `/click` in parallel; can be bypassed by copying the URL) vs **server-side 302** (records and redirects; guarantees tracking at the cost of latency/load).
2. **Metrics query**: same DB for writes and querying with GROUP BY (bad); events in Cassandra + Spark cron (MapReduce) + OLAP store (Redshift/Snowflake/BigQuery) (good; data minutes behind, spikes delay the batches; TSDB only for simple cases); **real-time streaming** (excellent): click processor -> Kafka/Kinesis -> Flink with event time and watermarks -> flush to OLAP every few seconds with minute windows ([[stream-processing]], [[olap-columnar-stores]]). Flink provides windows, watermarks, exactly-once and fault tolerance that are hard to rebuild with Kafka consumers.

## Deep dives
1. **Scale to 10k/s**: horizontal click processor behind an LB; stream sharded by AdId (Kinesis: 1 MB/s or 1000 records/s per shard); one Flink job per shard; managed OLAP or ClickHouse sharded by AdvertiserId. **Hot shards** ([[hot-keys]]): random suffix `AdId:0..N` on popular ads, Flink strips it and does an upsert with SUM (alternative: aggregate at query time).
2. **Do not lose clicks**: stream retention (~7 days) for replay; Flink checkpointing is not very critical with 1 min windows (loses ~1 min); **Lambda-style reconciliation**: archive raw events in S3 (Kafka Connect S3 Sink / Firehose), daily Spark batch, compare and correct the OLAP.
3. **Duplicates** ([[idempotency]]): userId in the payload (bad: requires login and breaks retargeting); unique **impression ID** generated by the Ad Placement Service, signed with HMAC (adId + secret), returned on the click; the processor verifies the signature, checks a cache (Redis Cluster with RDB/AOF) and discards duplicates; it writes to the stream before the cache (cache failure = occasional duplicates corrected in reconciliation, never lost clicks).
4. **Long-window queries**: pre-aggregation covers minutes; a nightly cron creates daily/weekly rollups in OLAP (storage traded for speed).

## Trade-offs and decisions
- Server-side 302: guaranteed tracking vs latency.
- Streaming vs batch: freshness vs simplicity; reconciliation provides correctness.
- Stream->cache ordering prioritizes not losing clicks.

## Variants
### Xu & Lam Vol. 2, ch. 6 (pp. 159-193)
Source: System Design Interview Vol. 2 (Xu & Lam, PDF), ch. 6 Ad Click Event Aggregation, pp. 159-193. It differs in scale (1,000 M clicks/day, 2 M ads, +30% yearly), in that the input is **logs on servers** (not clicks with a redirect) and in its focus: correctness for billing/RTB, with latency of minutes acceptable.
- **Requirements**: count clicks of an `ad_id` in the last M minutes; top 100 most clicked ads each minute (configurable parameters); filters by ip, user_id or country; late events, duplicates and recovery from failures. Event: ad_id, click_timestamp, user_id, ip, country.
- **Estimations**: 10^9/10^5 = **10,000 QPS** average, peak x5 = **50,000**; 0.1 KB/event = 100 GB/day, ~3 TB/month.
- **API**: `GET /v1/ads/{ad_id}/aggregated_count` (from, to, filter) and `GET /v1/ads/popular_ads` (count, window, filter); the filter goes as a parameter.
- **Data**: store **raw and aggregated**: raw data serves as backup, debugging and recomputation (moved to cold storage over time); the aggregates (per minute, with `filter_id`) are the active ones for querying. Raw in Cassandra/InfluxDB or S3 in columnar formats (ORC/Parquet/Avro); aggregates are time series with heavy reads and writes. Top N table (`update_time_minute`, `most_clicked_ads`).
- **Architecture**: log watcher -> queue (Kafka) -> **aggregation service** -> second queue -> database writers -> raw DB and aggregated DB -> query service. The second queue enables **end-to-end exactly-once** (atomic commit). Aggregation service as a **Map -> Aggregate -> Reduce** DAG (MapReduce): Map normalizes/filters and distributes by `ad_id`; Aggregate counts in memory per minute (and keeps a heap for the local top N); Reduce combines (e.g. 3 x top 3 -> global top 3). Filters via **star schema** (predefined dimensions such as country, at the cost of more buckets).
- **Streaming vs batch**: **Kappa** architecture (a single streaming path that also reprocesses history) versus Lambda (two paths and two codebases). **Recalculation service**: reads raw data and sends it to a dedicated instance of the aggregation service so as not to affect real time.
- **Time**: *event time* (more accurate, the client can lie) vs *processing time* (reliable but imprecise with delays); event time + **watermark** is recommended (extended window, e.g. 15 s adjustable; greater accuracy in exchange for latency); very late events are corrected with reconciliation. **Windows**: tumbling (1 min, for counts) and sliding (last M minutes, for top N).
- **Delivery**: exactly-once recommended (small differences = millions of dollars). Duplicates from the client (fraud goes to another component) or aggregator crash (Kafka offset not committed): saving the offset in HDFS/S3 before sending loses data; after the ack it still duplicates; the solution is a **distributed transaction** encompassing downstream send, offset save and ack ([[exactly-once-processing]]).
- **Scaling**: queues, aggregation and DB independent. Consumers: rebalancing (slow with hundreds; do it in off-peak hours). Brokers: `ad_id` as hashing key, preallocate partitions, topics split by geography or business type. Aggregation: multithreading by ad_id or deploy on YARN (most used). Cassandra scales with virtual nodes like [[consistent-hashing]].
- **Hotspots**: ads with large budgets overload nodes; request more nodes from the resource manager, split the events and reduce; more advanced techniques: Global-Local Aggregation, Split Distinct Aggregation.
- **Fault tolerance**: the state is in memory; **snapshots** of the system state (upstream offset + top N of the last M minutes) to recover on a new node and replay from Kafka only what came after.
- **Monitoring and correctness**: latency between stages, queue size/lag (records-lag in Kafka), node resources. **Reconciliation**: end-of-day batch that sorts events by event time per partition and compares with the real-time result (there is no third party to reconcile with).
- **Alternative**: Hive + Elasticsearch layer and aggregation in OLAP (ClickHouse, Druid) with a risk control engine.
